## 1516

> SCENE (photo): The story.
> beer-hook | 1516, or 1302? | color

[curious] The story says 1516 gave Germany its beer purity law. The paper trail shows beer rules from 1302.

> SCENE (term): April 1516.
> Ingolstadt, April 1516 | Dukes Wilhelm IV and Ludwig X | A general code of law, with beer as one small section (Historisches Lexikon Bayerns)

[calm] April 1516, Ingolstadt. Two Bavarian dukes agree a general code of law with their estates. Beer is one small section.

> SCENE (bignums): The price first.
> The beer price in the 1516 code (per Maß)
> 1 penny | winter beer, Munich @ one Munich penny
> 2 pennies | March beer @ two

[calm] First comes the price. One Munich penny per Maß in winter. Two for the March beer.

> SCENE (receipt): The sentence.
> bg | bg-typewriter
> Landesordnung 1516 (translation, Historisches Lexikon Bayerns) | The famous sentence
> For no beer ==anything else== shall be used | @ anything else
> but ==barley, hops and water== alone | @ barley, hops and water

[calm] Only then the famous sentence: for beer, nothing may be used but barley, hops and water.

> SCENE (stamp): Three things.
> Three things. No yeast. | red

[curious] Three things. Yeast isn't one of them.

> SCENE (photo): The foam.
> yeast-foam | Beer still needed yeast | color

[amused] Beer still needed yeast. The 1516 sentence just didn't list it.

> SCENE (receipt): Older rules.
> bg | bg-archive
> Historisches Lexikon Bayerns, with archive shelf marks | Before 1516
> Nuremberg, ==around 1300==: barley only | @ thirteen hundred
> Munich, ==1487==: hops, barley, water | @ fourteen eighty-seven

[calm] And it wasn't first. Nuremberg had a barley-only rule around thirteen hundred. Munich listed hops, barley and water in fourteen eighty-seven.

## The word

> SCENE (term): Word lab.
> Bier | Old High German bior, 9th century | Origin: unexplained (DWDS, Pfeifer)

[curious] Word lab. Bier is old: bior, in the ninth century. Where it comes from, the dictionary says, is unexplained.

> SCENE (photo): Three guesses.
> beer-guesses | Latin drink, swelling foam, or brew | color

[amused] Three guesses: a Latin word for drink, an old root for swelling foam, or a cousin of brew.

> SCENE (line): The word.
> And the word Reinheitsgebot?

[curious] And the word Reinheitsgebot? Much younger than 1516.

> SCENE (receipt): Two dates.
> bg | bg-typewriter2
> Two Bavarian offices | When was the word first used?
> Historisches Lexikon Bayerns: ==1909==, in Berlin | @ nineteen oh nine
> Food safety office LGL: ==4 March 1918== | @ nineteen eighteen

[calm] A Bavarian lexicon says it first appears in 1909, in Berlin. Bavaria's food safety office says 1918. Two sources, two dates.

> SCENE (photo): Wheat.
> beer-wheat | Wheat was for bakers | color

[warmly] Wheat was kept for bakers. Wheat beer was basically banned, except for families with a duke's permission.

> SCENE (term): The list grew.
> 1551 and 1616 | coriander and laurel; then salt, juniper, caraway | The rule was changed over time (Historisches Lexikon Bayerns)

[calm] And the list grew. In 1551, coriander and laurel. In 1616, salt, juniper and caraway seed, in moderation.

## Today

> SCENE (photo): The law today.
> brewery | Today: Vorläufiges Biergesetz, § 9 | color

[calm] Today's law: bottom-fermented beer, only barley malt, hops, yeast and water. Top-fermented may add other malt and sugar.

> SCENE (receipt): Is it safer?
> bg | bg-berlin
> Bavarian food safety office (LGL) | Does it make beer safer?
> Hops, ==acidity, alcohol and carbon dioxide== | studies show they hold back germs @ acidity

[curious] Does it make beer safer? Bavaria's food office says studies show hops, acidity, alcohol and carbon dioxide hold back germs.
