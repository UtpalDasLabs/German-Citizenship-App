[calm] On Wednesday the eighteenth of November, schools and many offices in Saxony are closed. Everywhere else in Germany, it's a normal working day.

> SCENE (states): Saxony lights up.
> Sachsen @ Buß

[calm] It's [[Buß- und Bettag]], a Protestant day of repentance and prayer. Until the nineteen-nineties, it was a public holiday in almost all of Germany.

> SCENE (photo): Church pews in the light.
> church |  | color

[calm] Then, in 1995, Germany introduced long-term care insurance. To help employers pay for it, the states gave up a working holiday, and this was the one they chose. All except one: Saxony.

> SCENE (photo): Dresden from above; coins stacking up.
> dresden |  | color
> coins | Saxony kept the day off, and pays for it. | color key @ In return

[warmly] Saxony kept its day off. In return, employees there pay a bigger share of the care insurance than anywhere else, about half a percentage point of their wages. So in Saxony, this holiday isn't quite free.
