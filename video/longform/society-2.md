## Cold open

> SCENE (fact): The march to Hambach Castle, 1832.
> 27 MAY 1832 | Hambacher Fest | About 30,000 people march to Hambach Castle for a united, free Germany, under black, red and gold.

[dramatic] In May 1832, around thirty thousand people marched up a hill to a ruined castle near Neustadt, in the Palatinate. Students, craftsmen, women, farmers. They wanted a united Germany, with freedom of the press and a say in their own government. And they carried a flag in three colours: black, red and gold.

{Q214}

> SCENE (question): Question card #214. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Welche Farben hat die deutsche Flagge?]] What are the colours of the German flag?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[schwarz-rot-gold]].

[dramatic] The trap is [[rot-weiß-schwarz]], close to the flag of the old German Empire, which was black, white and red. Black, red, gold is the flag of democracy, from Hambach in 1832 to the Federal Republic today.

## Anthem and state

{Q40}

> SCENE (question): Question card #40. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Mit welchen Worten beginnt die deutsche Nationalhymne?]] What are the first words of the national anthem?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[Einigkeit und Recht und Freiheit]]. Unity and justice and freedom. It's the third verse of an old song; only that verse is the anthem.

[dramatic] The wrong answers are all real songs, just not this one. [[Freude schöner Götterfunken]] is Beethoven's Ode to Joy, the anthem of Europe. [[Deutschland einig Vaterland]] is from the anthem of East Germany. And [[Völker, hört die Signale]] is the socialist Internationale.

{Q27}

> SCENE (question): Question card #27. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Deutschland ist …]] Germany is…

[countdown] [dramatic] [[ein Bundesstaat.]] A federal state: sixteen states, one federation. Not socialist, not a dictatorship, not a monarchy.

{Q50}

> SCENE (question): Question card #50. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Die Wirtschaftsform in Deutschland nennt man …]] Germany's economic system is called…

[countdown] [dramatic] [[soziale Marktwirtschaft.]] The social market economy: markets with a safety net. Every option with "Zentral" or "Plan" in it is the opposite.

{Q46}

> SCENE (question): Question card #46. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Der deutsche Staat hat viele Aufgaben. Welche Aufgabe gehört dazu?]] Which task belongs to the state?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[Er baut Straßen und Schulen.]] It builds roads and schools. It doesn't sell food, make cars, or hand out free newspapers. In a market economy, companies do that.

{Q67}

> SCENE (question): Question card #67. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Was ist in Deutschland vor allem eine Aufgabe der Bundesländer?]] What is mainly a task of the states?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[Schulpolitik]], education. Defence and foreign policy belong to the federation.

{Q92}

> SCENE (question): Question card #92. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Was bedeutet die Abkürzung CSU in Deutschland?]] What does CSU stand for?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[Christlich Soziale Union]]. The Christian Social Union. It only exists in Bavaria. Everywhere else, its sister party, the CDU, stands for election instead.

## Faith

{Q33}

> SCENE (question): Question card #33. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Welche Aussage ist richtig? In Deutschland …]] Which statement is true? In Germany…

[countdown] [dramatic] [[sind Staat und Religionsgemeinschaften voneinander getrennt.]] State and religious communities are separate. There's no state church. The state does cooperate with religious communities, collecting church tax, for example, but it doesn't belong to any of them.

{Q182}

> SCENE (question): Question card #182. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Wie heißt das jüdische Gebetshaus?]] What is a Jewish house of prayer called?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[Synagoge]]. A mosque is for Muslims, a church or basilica for Christians.

{Q118}

> SCENE (question): Question card #118. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Wer darf bei den rund 40 jüdischen Makkabi-Sportvereinen Mitglied werden?]] Who can join the forty or so Jewish Maccabi sports clubs?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[alle Menschen]], everyone. Not only Jews, not only Israelis, not only religious people. Anyone who wants to play.

## Who lives in Germany

{Q213}

> SCENE (question): Question card #213. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Wie viele Einwohner hat Deutschland?]] How many people live in Germany?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[84 Millionen]], eighty-four million. Official counts in recent years have been close to that, between eighty-three and eighty-four million. For the exam, remember eighty-four.

{Q225}

> SCENE (question): Question card #225. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[In welchem anderen Land gibt es eine große deutschsprachige Bevölkerung?]] Which other country has a large German-speaking population?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[Österreich]], Austria. German is spoken in Switzerland, Liechtenstein and Luxembourg too, but none of those are among the options.

{Q297}

> SCENE (question): Question card #297. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Aus welchem Land sind die meisten Migrantinnen und Migranten nach Deutschland gekommen?]] Where did most migrants to Germany come from?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[Türkei]], Turkey. It began with the guest workers of the 1960s; today almost three million people in Germany have Turkish roots, more than from any other country.

## The year in festivals

> SCENE (list): The year in four festivals.
> Rosenmontag | costumes and masks @ Rosenmontag
> Ostern | painted eggs @ Ostern
> Pfingsten | a Christian holiday @ Pfingsten
> Advent | four weeks before Christmas @ Advent

[dramatic] Let's finish with the calendar. The exam asks about four festivals: [[Rosenmontag]], [[Ostern]], [[Pfingsten]] and [[Advent]].

{Q296}

> SCENE (question): Question card #296. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[In Deutschland nennt man die letzten vier Wochen vor Weihnachten …]] The four weeks before Christmas are called…

[countdown] [dramatic] [[die Adventszeit.]] Advent. One candle on the wreath each Sunday, and Christmas markets everywhere.

{Q271}

> SCENE (question): Question card #271. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Was ist in Deutschland ein Brauch zu Weihnachten?]] Which is a Christmas custom?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[einen Tannenbaum schmücken]], decorating a Christmas tree. Hiding eggs is Easter, costumes are carnival, and pumpkins are Halloween.

{Q264}

> SCENE (question): Question card #264. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Zu welchem Fest tragen Menschen in Deutschland bunte Kostüme und Masken?]] When do people wear colourful costumes and masks?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[am Rosenmontag]], the Monday of carnival, especially in the Rhineland: Cologne, Düsseldorf, Mainz. Not at the Oktoberfest, where it's lederhosen and dirndls.

{Q293}

> SCENE (question): Question card #293. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Was ist in Deutschland ein Brauch zu Ostern?]] An Easter custom?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[Eier bemalen]], painting eggs. Rockets go up on New Year's Eve, not at Easter.

{Q294}

> SCENE (question): Question card #294. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Pfingsten ist ein …]] Pentecost is a…

[countdown] [dramatic] [[christlicher Feiertag.]] A Christian holiday, fifty days after Easter. Not a Bavarian custom, and not a day of mourning.
