## Cold open

> SCENE (stat): Two votes on one ballot.
> 1 | Erststimme: a person @ first vote
> 2 | Zweitstimme: a party @ second vote

[curious] On a German ballot you have two votes. The first, the [[Erststimme]], is for a person: the candidate for your area. The second, the [[Zweitstimme]], is for a party. And the second one decides how many seats each party gets.

[warm] This lesson follows those votes into the Bundestag: parties, the five percent hurdle, coalitions, government and opposition. Twenty more questions.

## Why parties

{Q41}

> SCENE (question): Question card #41.

[[Warum gibt es in einer Demokratie mehr als eine Partei?]] Why is there more than one party in a democracy?

[[weil dadurch die unterschiedlichen Meinungen der Bürgerinnen und Bürger vertreten werden]]. Because citizens have different opinions, and each deserves a voice. It's not about competition in business, and not about stopping demonstrations.

{Q93}

> SCENE (question): Question card #93.

[[Je mehr "Zweitstimmen" eine Partei bei einer Bundestagswahl bekommt, desto …]] The more second votes a party gets, the…

[[mehr Sitze erhält die Partei im Parlament.]] The more seats it gets in parliament. That's why the second vote matters most.

{Q113}

> SCENE (question): Question card #113.

[[Wahlen in Deutschland gewinnt die Partei, die …]] The party that wins an election is the one that…

[[die meisten Stimmen bekommt.]] gets the most votes. Not the most votes from men, or from workers. Every vote counts the same.

## The five percent hurdle

> SCENE (stat): The hurdle.
> 5 % | of second votes to enter the Bundestag @ five percent

[curious] There's one big rule for getting into the Bundestag at all: a party needs at least five percent of the second votes.

{Q117}

> SCENE (question): Question card #117.

[[Wie viel Prozent der Zweitstimmen müssen Parteien mindestens bekommen, um in den Deutschen Bundestag gewählt zu werden?]] How many percent? [[5%]]. There are narrow exceptions, for parties that win three constituencies and for national minorities, but for the exam: five.

{Q121}

> SCENE (question): Question card #121.

[[Eine Partei möchte in den Deutschen Bundestag. Sie muss aber einen Mindestanteil an Wählerstimmen haben. Das heißt …]] The minimum share of votes is called… [[5%-Hürde.]] The five percent hurdle.

{Q123}

> SCENE (question): Question card #123.

[[Was ist in Deutschland die "5%-Hürde"?]] What is it? [[Mindestanteil an Wählerstimmen, um ins Parlament zu kommen]]. The minimum share of votes to get into parliament. It has nothing to do with attendance.

{Q127}

> SCENE (question): Question card #127.

[[Warum gibt es die 5%-Hürde im Wahlgesetz der Bundesrepublik Deutschland? Es gibt sie, weil …]] Why does it exist?

[[viele kleine Parteien die Regierungsbildung erschweren.]] Because many small parties make it hard to form a government. The Weimar Republic had dozens of parties in parliament, and governments kept collapsing. The hurdle is a lesson from that.

{Q120}

> SCENE (question): Question card #120.

[[Das Wahlsystem in Deutschland ist ein …]] Germany's electoral system is a… [[Mehrheits- und Verhältniswahlrecht.]] A mix of majority and proportional voting: the first vote picks a person by majority, the second shares out seats by proportion. The other options are historical, unequal systems.

## Coalitions

{Q31}

> SCENE (question): Question card #31.

[[Die Zusammenarbeit von Parteien zur Bildung einer Regierung nennt man in Deutschland …]] When parties work together to form a government, it's called a… [[Koalition.]] Coalition. A [[Fraktion]] is something else: we'll get to that.

{Q103}

> SCENE (question): Question card #103.
> @ von SPD, FDP

[[Was wird in Deutschland als "Ampelkoalition" bezeichnet?]] What is a "traffic light coalition"?

[[von SPD, FDP und den Grünen in einer Regierung]]. Red for the SPD, yellow for the FDP, green for the Greens: like a traffic light. That coalition governed from 2021 to 2024.

{Q73}

> SCENE (question): Question card #73.
> @ CDU und CSU

[[Die beiden größten Fraktionen im Deutschen Bundestag heißen zurzeit …]] The two largest groups in the Bundestag at the moment? [[CDU und CSU, und die AfD.]] That's the result of the February 2025 election. If there's a new election, this answer can change.

{Q76}

> SCENE (question): Question card #76.

[[Was bedeutet die Abkürzung CDU in Deutschland?]] [[Christlich Demokratische Union]]. The Christian Democratic Union.

{Q78}

> SCENE (question): Question card #78.

[[Was bedeutet die Abkürzung SPD?]] [[Sozialdemokratische Partei Deutschlands]]. The Social Democratic Party. Careful: not "sozialistisch". Social democratic.

{Q79}

> SCENE (question): Question card #79.

[[Was bedeutet die Abkürzung FDP in Deutschland?]] [[Freie Demokratische Partei]]. The Free Democratic Party, the liberals.

## Inside the Bundestag

{Q13}

> SCENE (question): Question card #13.

[[Im Parlament steht der Begriff "Opposition" für …]] In parliament, the "opposition" means…

[[alle Abgeordneten, die nicht zu der Regierungspartei oder den Regierungsparteien gehören.]] All the members who don't belong to the governing parties.

{Q88}

> SCENE (question): Question card #88.

[[Die parlamentarische Opposition im Deutschen Bundestag …]] What does the opposition do? [[kontrolliert die Regierung.]] It holds the government to account: asks questions, criticises, offers alternatives.

{Q89}

> SCENE (question): Question card #89.

[[Wie nennt man in Deutschland die Vereinigung von Abgeordneten einer Partei im Parlament?]] What's the group of one party's members in parliament called? [[Fraktion]]. A parliamentary group. Not to be confused with a coalition, which is between parties.

{Q98}

> SCENE (question): Question card #98.

[[Wenn Abgeordnete im Deutschen Bundestag ihre Fraktion wechseln, …]] If members switch parliamentary groups…

[[kann die Regierung ihre Mehrheit verlieren.]] The government can lose its majority. Members of the Bundestag are free to do that: they answer to their conscience, not to the party.

{Q57}

> SCENE (question): Question card #57.
> @ ein Abgeordneter der stärksten

[[Wer wird meistens zur Präsidentin oder zum Präsidenten des Deutschen Bundestages gewählt?]] Who usually becomes President of the Bundestag?

[[ein Abgeordneter der stärksten Fraktion]]: a member of the largest parliamentary group. Not the oldest member, and not a former chancellor.

{Q128}

> SCENE (question): Question card #128.

[[Parlamentsmitglieder, die von den Bürgerinnen und Bürgern gewählt werden, nennt man …]] Members of parliament elected by the people are called… [[Abgeordnete.]] Not ambassadors, not chancellors.

## What to remember for the exam

> SCENE (list): Recap board. Three cards appear one by one.
> Zweitstimme | → decides the seats @ One.
> 5%-Hürde | → keeps parliament workable @ Two.
> Koalition, Fraktion | → between parties, within one @ Three.

[warm] Three things to take away. One: the second vote decides how many seats a party gets.

Two: below five percent, a party usually doesn't get in, so that parliament can form governments.

Three: a coalition is between parties; a [[Fraktion]] is the group within one.
