## Cold open

> SCENE (quote): One sentence that turns a state upside down.
> Alle Staatsgewalt geht vom Volke aus. | All state power comes from the people. | Grundgesetz, Artikel 20

[dramatic] For most of history, power flowed downwards: from a king, an emperor, a party, to the people. Germany's Basic Law turns that around in one sentence: [[Alle Staatsgewalt geht vom Volke aus.]] All state power comes from the people.

[dramatic] And a second idea goes with it: the state that holds that power has to obey the law too. Twenty exam questions in this lesson are about those two ideas, democracy and the rule of law.

## The state obeys the law

{Q3}

> SCENE (question): Question card #3. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.
> @ Everyone has to follow

[dramatic] [[Deutschland ist ein Rechtsstaat. Was ist damit gemeint?]] Germany is a Rechtsstaat. What does that mean?

[countdown] [dramatic] Everyone has to follow the laws: the people who live here, and the state itself. Not only Germans; everyone. And courts apply the laws; they don't make them.

{Q53}

> SCENE (question): Question card #53. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] The same idea, shorter: [[Was bedeutet "Rechtsstaat" in Deutschland?]]

[countdown] [dramatic] [[Der Staat muss die Gesetze einhalten.]] The state must keep to the law.

[dramatic] Two traps here play with the word "Recht". "Der Staat hat Recht": the state is always right. No. And "es gibt nur rechte Parteien": only right-wing parties. Also no. In [[Rechtsstaat]], "Recht" means law.

{Q34}

> SCENE (question): Question card #34. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Was ist Deutschland nicht?]] What is Germany not?

[dramatic] Germany is a democracy, a Rechtsstaat and a social state.

[countdown] [dramatic] [[eine Monarchie]]. The last German emperor gave up the throne in 1918.

{Q52}

> SCENE (question): Question card #52. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Was bedeutet "Volkssouveränität"? Alle Staatsgewalt geht vom ...]] What does popular sovereignty mean? All state power comes from the…

[countdown] [dramatic] [[Volke aus.]] The people. Not the Bundestag, not the constitutional court, and certainly not the Prussian king.

{Q61}

> SCENE (question): Question card #61. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] The same question, other options: [[Was bedeutet "Volkssouveränität"?]]

[countdown] [dramatic] [[Die Staatsgewalt geht vom Volke aus.]] Whenever you see [[Volkssouveränität]], look for [[Volk]].

## What the state may never do

{Q4}

> SCENE (question): Question card #4. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Welches Recht gehört zu den Grundrechten in Deutschland?]] Which right is a basic right in Germany?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[Meinungsfreiheit]], freedom of opinion. The other options are about taking the law into your own hands: [[Faustrecht]], the law of the fist; [[Selbstjustiz]], vigilante justice. And owning a gun is not a basic right in Germany.

{Q10}

> SCENE (question): Question card #10. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Was ist mit dem deutschen Grundgesetz vereinbar?]] What is compatible with the Basic Law?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[die Geldstrafe]], a fine. Corporal punishment and torture are forbidden. And Article 102 says it in five words: [[Die Todesstrafe ist abgeschafft.]] The death penalty is abolished.

{Q20}

> SCENE (question): Question card #20. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Eine Partei in Deutschland verfolgt das Ziel, eine Diktatur zu errichten. Sie ist dann …]] A party wants to set up a dictatorship. It is then…

[countdown] [dramatic] [[verfassungswidrig.]] Unconstitutional. And as you know from the Basic Rights lesson, only the constitutional court can ban it.

{Q51}

> SCENE (question): Question card #51. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Zu einem demokratischen Rechtsstaat gehört es nicht, dass …]] What does not belong in a democratic Rechtsstaat?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[Menschen von einer Privatpolizei ohne Grund verhaftet werden.]] People being arrested for no reason by a private police force. Criticising the government, demonstrating peacefully, and being arrested after committing a crime: all normal parts of a Rechtsstaat.

{Q111}

> SCENE (question): Question card #111. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Welche Handlungen mit Bezug auf den Staat Israel sind in Deutschland verboten?]] Which acts concerning Israel are forbidden in Germany?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[der öffentliche Aufruf zur Vernichtung Israels]], publicly calling for Israel's destruction. That's incitement, and it's a crime. Criticising Israel's government, discussing its politics, hanging an Israeli flag on your own property: all allowed.

## Free elections

{Q5}

> SCENE (question): Question card #5. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.
> @ weder beeinflusst

[dramatic] [[Wahlen in Deutschland sind frei. Was bedeutet das?]] Elections in Germany are free. What does that mean?

[countdown] [dramatic] The voter may [[weder beeinflusst noch zu einer bestimmten Stimmabgabe gezwungen werden]], neither be influenced nor forced to vote a certain way, and must not suffer any disadvantage because of the vote.

[dramatic] Careful with the option about prison: "only people who have never been in prison may vote". That's false, and it has nothing to do with free elections. Some study materials have this one marked wrongly, so learn it from the logic: free means free from pressure.

{Q119}

> SCENE (question): Question card #119. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Wahlen in Deutschland sind frei. Was bedeutet das?]] The same question, in a second version.

[countdown] [dramatic] [[Jede Person kann ohne Zwang entscheiden, ob sie wählen möchte und wen sie wählen möchte.]] Everyone decides without pressure whether to vote, and whom to vote for. Voting in Germany isn't compulsory, and nobody gets to tell you how.

## Who makes the laws

{Q42}

> SCENE (question): Question card #42. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Wer beschließt in Deutschland ein neues Gesetz?]] Who passes a new law?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[das Parlament]], parliament. The government can propose laws, courts apply them, police enforce them. But only parliament passes them.

{Q74}

> SCENE (question): Question card #74. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Wie heißt das Parlament für ganz Deutschland?]] What is the parliament for all of Germany called?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[Bundestag]].

[dramatic] The traps: the [[Volkskammer]] was the parliament of East Germany. The [[Bundesversammlung]] meets only to elect the president. And the [[Bundesgerichtshof]] is a court.

{Q86}

> SCENE (question): Question card #86. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.
> @ die Bundesversammlung

[dramatic] [[Wer wählt in Deutschland den Bundespräsidenten?]] Who elects the federal president?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[die Bundesversammlung]]: all the members of the Bundestag, plus the same number of delegates from the states.

{Q58}

> SCENE (question): Question card #58. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.
> @ den Bundespräsidenten

[dramatic] [[Wer ernennt in Deutschland die Minister der Bundesregierung?]] Who appoints the federal ministers?

[countdown] [dramatic] The federal president, [[den Bundespräsidenten]], on the chancellor's proposal. Appointing people to office is one of the president's formal jobs.

{Q55}

> SCENE (question): Question card #55. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.
> @ den Bundestagssitz

[dramatic] [[Was zeigt dieses Bild?]] What does this picture show? The exam shows a building with a glass dome.

[countdown] [dramatic] [[den Bundestagssitz in Berlin]], the seat of the Bundestag. Not the constitutional court, which is in Karlsruhe.

## Carrying out the law

{Q63}

> SCENE (question): Question card #63. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Was gehört in Deutschland nicht zur Exekutive?]] What is not part of the executive?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[die Gerichte]], the courts. The courts are the judiciary. The police, the tax office and the ministries carry out the law: that's the executive.

{Q77}

> SCENE (question): Question card #77. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Was ist die Bundeswehr?]] What is the Bundeswehr?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[die deutsche Armee]], the German armed forces, founded in 1955. Not the police, and certainly not a port.

{Q148}

> SCENE (question): Question card #148. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Was ist eine Aufgabe der Polizei in Deutschland?]] What is a task of the police?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[die Einhaltung von Gesetzen zu überwachen]], making sure laws are kept. Defending the country is the Bundeswehr's job. Passing laws is parliament's. And listening in on citizens is not a police task; it needs a court's permission in specific cases.

## What to remember for the exam

> SCENE (list): Recap board. Three cards appear one by one.
> Rechtsstaat | → the state obeys the law too @ One.
> Volkssouveränität | → power comes from the people @ Two.
> Parlament | → passes the laws @ Three.

[dramatic] Three things to take away. One: [[Rechtsstaat]]. The state obeys the law, like everyone else.

[dramatic] Two: [[Volkssouveränität]]. All power comes from the people.

[dramatic] Three: parliament makes the laws, the executive carries them out, the courts judge.
