## Cold open

> SCENE (term): The country's full name, word by word.
> Bundesrepublik Deutschland | Federal Republic of Germany

[curious] Germany's full name is three words long: [[Bundesrepublik Deutschland]]. And each word tells you something about how the country works.

[warm] "Bund" means federal: sixteen states that share power with the centre. "Republik" means there's no king; the people choose who governs them. This lesson takes the country apart, piece by piece. Twenty-six exam questions, from the eagle on the coat of arms to the office that handles your complaints.

{Q212}

> SCENE (question): Question card #212.

[[Wie heißt Deutschland mit vollem Namen?]] What is Germany's full name? [[Bundesrepublik Deutschland]]. Not "Bundesstaat", not "Bundesländer", not "Bundesbezirk". They all start the same way, so read to the end of the word.

{Q22}

> SCENE (question): Question card #22.

[[Was für eine Staatsform hat Deutschland?]] What form of state does Germany have? [[Republik]]. A republic. Not a monarchy, not a principality, and certainly not a dictatorship.

## Sixteen states

> SCENE (states): All sixteen states light up.
> ALLE | 16 Bundesländer @ sixteen

[curious] Let's start with the "Bund": the federation. Germany is made of sixteen states, the [[Bundesländer]].

{Q24}

> SCENE (question): Question card #24.

[[Wie viele Bundesländer hat die Bundesrepublik Deutschland?]] How many states? [[16]]. Before reunification in 1990 there were eleven in the West; five more joined from the East.

{Q25}

> SCENE (question): Question card #25.

[firm] A [[kein]] question: [[Was ist kein Bundesland der Bundesrepublik Deutschland?]] Which is not a German state?

[[Elsass-Lothringen]]. Alsace-Lorraine. It was German between 1871 and 1918, but today it's part of France. The other three, North Rhine-Westphalia, Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania and Saxony-Anhalt, are all real states, even if their names are a mouthful.

{Q39}

> SCENE (question): Question card #39.

[[Was hat jedes deutsche Bundesland?]] What does every state have?

[[eine eigene Regierung]], its own government. But not its own currency, its own army, or its own foreign minister. Those belong to the federation.

{Q49}

> SCENE (question): Question card #49.

[[Wer bestimmt in Deutschland die Schulpolitik?]] Who decides education policy?

[[die Bundesländer]], the states. That's why school systems differ from one state to the next, and why moving from Bavaria to Berlin can mean a different school system for your children.

## Three powers

> SCENE (list): Three powers, three cards.
> Legislative | makes the laws: the parliaments @ Legislative
> Exekutive | carries them out: governments and offices @ Exekutive
> Judikative | judges: the courts @ Judikative

[curious] Now the second big idea: no single body holds all the power. The state's power is split three ways. The [[Legislative]] makes laws: that's parliament. The [[Exekutive]] carries them out: the government and its offices. And the [[Judikative]] judges: the courts.

{Q32}

> SCENE (question): Question card #32.

[firm] [[Was ist keine staatliche Gewalt in Deutschland?]] What is not a state power?

[[Presse]], the press. In German the three powers are called [[Gesetzgebung]], [[Regierung]] and [[Rechtsprechung]]. The press is important for democracy, people even call it the fourth power, but it isn't part of the state. That's the whole point: it watches the state.

{Q54}

> SCENE (question): Question card #54.

The same question with the Latin words: [[Legislative]], [[Judikative]], [[Exekutive]]... and [[Direktive]]. Direktive sounds official, but it's made up. It's the one that doesn't belong.

> SCENE (photo): Schloss Bellevue, the president's official residence in Berlin.
> bellevue | Schloss Bellevue, Berlin: the federal president's official seat | color

[curious] At the top sit two people the exam asks about by name. The first is the head of state.

{Q84}

> SCENE (question): Question card #84.

[[Welche Hauptaufgabe hat die deutsche Bundespräsidentin oder der deutsche Bundespräsident?]] What is the federal president's main task?

[[repräsentiert das Land.]] To represent the country. The president doesn't govern and doesn't write laws. That's the chancellor and parliament.

{Q75}

> SCENE (question): Question card #75.

[[Wie heißt Deutschlands heutiges Staatsoberhaupt?]] Who is Germany's head of state today? [[Frank-Walter Steinmeier]]. He has been president since 2017. Joachim Gauck was the president before him; Bärbel Bas and Bodo Ramelow are politicians, but neither has been head of state.

[serious] One warning: Steinmeier's second term ends in March 2027. When a new president is elected, this answer changes. The app is updated when it does.

> SCENE (photo): The Reichstag building, seat of the Bundestag.
> reichstag | The Reichstag building, Berlin: seat of the Bundestag | color

The second person runs the government, and is elected by the [[Bundestag]].

{Q72}

> SCENE (question): Question card #72.

[[Wie heißt der jetzige Bundeskanzler von Deutschland?]] Who is the current chancellor? [[Friedrich Merz]], chancellor since May 2025. Gerhard Schröder and Angela Merkel were chancellors before him. And Ursula von der Leyen is the President of the European Commission, in Brussels.

## Eagle and coat of arms

{Q29}

> SCENE (question): Question card #29.

[[Welches Tier ist das Wappentier der Bundesrepublik Deutschland?]] Which animal is on Germany's coat of arms? [[Adler]], the eagle. The [[Bundesadler]]. You've seen it above the seats in the Bundestag.

{Q21}

> SCENE (question): Question card #21. The four coats of arms; the black eagle on gold lights up.
> @ black eagle

[[Welches ist das Wappen der Bundesrepublik Deutschland?]] Which one is the coat of arms of Germany? Look for the black eagle on a gold shield, with a red beak and red claws.

## Three levels

> SCENE (list): Three levels of the state.
> Bund | the federation @ Bund
> Länder | the sixteen states @ Länder
> Gemeinden | towns and municipalities @ Gemeinden

[warm] Germany is run at three levels. The federation, the [[Bund]]. The states, the [[Länder]]. And at the bottom, closest to you: your town, the [[Gemeinden]].

{Q69}

> SCENE (question): Question card #69.

[[Die Bundesrepublik Deutschland hat einen dreistufigen Verwaltungsaufbau. Wie heißt die unterste politische Stufe?]] What's the lowest level?

[[Gemeinden]], municipalities. The traps are people and offices that work at that level, a city council, a district administrator, but the level itself is called [[Gemeinde]].

{Q56}

> SCENE (question): Question card #56.

[[Welches Amt gehört in Deutschland zur Gemeindeverwaltung?]] Which office belongs to the town administration?

[[Ordnungsamt]], the public order office: parking, noise, markets, and a lot more. The [[Finanzamt]] belongs to the state, the [[Auswärtiges Amt]] to the federation, and the [[Pfarramt]] to a church.

{Q276}

> SCENE (question): Question card #276.
> @ beim Behördenleiter beschweren

[[Was sollten Sie tun, wenn Sie von Ihrem Ansprechpartner in einer deutschen Behörde schlecht behandelt werden?]] What should you do if someone at an office treats you badly?

You can complain to the head of that office: [[Ich kann mich beim Behördenleiter beschweren.]] You don't have to put up with it, and threatening the person is never the answer.

{Q283}

> SCENE (question): Question card #283.

[[Was tun Sie, wenn Sie eine falsche Rechnung von einer deutschen Behörde bekommen?]] What do you do if an office sends you a wrong bill?

[[Ich lege Widerspruch bei der Behörde ein.]] You lodge an objection, a [[Widerspruch]], with the office that sent it. Don't ignore it, and don't just send it back. For a tax assessment, the same idea is called an [[Einspruch]].

{Q134}

> SCENE (question): Question card #134.

[[Man will die Buslinie abschaffen, mit der Sie immer zur Arbeit fahren. Was können Sie machen, um die Buslinie zu erhalten?]] Your bus line is being cut. What can you do?

[[Ich beteilige mich an einer Bürgerinitiative für die Erhaltung der Buslinie oder gründe selber eine Initiative.]] Join a citizens' initiative, or start one. That's democracy at the level of the [[Gemeinde]]. The tax office and the forestry office won't save your bus.

{Q132}

> SCENE (question): Question card #132.

[[Viele Menschen in Deutschland arbeiten in ihrer Freizeit ehrenamtlich. Was bedeutet das?]] Many people do [[ehrenamtlich]] work. What does that mean?

[[Sie arbeiten freiwillig und unbezahlt in Vereinen und Verbänden.]] Voluntary and unpaid, in clubs and associations. The volunteer fire brigade, the football club coach, the food bank. If it's paid, it isn't [[Ehrenamt]].

## The economic miracle

{Q23}

> SCENE (question): Question card #23.

[curious] How do most people in Germany earn a living? [[In Deutschland sind die meisten Erwerbstätigen …]]

[[bei einer Firma oder Behörde beschäftigt.]] Employed by a company or a public authority. About nine in ten working people are employees; only around one in ten works for themselves.

{Q183}

> SCENE (question): Question card #183.

[[Wann war in der Bundesrepublik Deutschland das "Wirtschaftswunder"?]] When was the "economic miracle"?

In the [[50er Jahre]], the 1950s. A few years after the war, West Germany's economy grew so fast that people called it a miracle. And it grew so fast that there weren't enough workers.

{Q299}

> SCENE (question): Question card #299.
> @ Gastarbeiter

[[Ausländische Arbeitnehmerinnen und Arbeitnehmer, die in den 50er und 60er Jahren von der Bundesrepublik Deutschland angeworben wurden, nannte man …]] Workers recruited from abroad in the fifties and sixties were called…

[[Gastarbeiter]], guest workers. The name assumed they would leave again. Many stayed, raised families, and became part of Germany.

{Q300}

> SCENE (question): Question card #300.

[[Aus welchem Land kamen die ersten Gastarbeiterinnen und Gastarbeiter in die Bundesrepublik Deutschland?]] Where did the first guest workers come from?

[[Italien]], Italy. The first agreement was signed in 1955. Spain and Greece followed in 1960, Turkey in 1961, Portugal in 1964. Turkey is the trap: it sent the most workers, but it wasn't the first.

## Jewish life in Germany

> SCENE (photo): The New Synagogue on Oranienburger Straße, Berlin.
> synagogue | The New Synagogue, Berlin | color

[warm] The last three questions are about Jewish life in Germany, which is far older than most people think.

{Q59}

> SCENE (question): Question card #59.

[[Vor wie vielen Jahren gab es erstmals eine jüdische Gemeinde auf dem Gebiet des heutigen Deutschlands?]] How long ago was there first a Jewish community in what is now Germany?

[[vor etwa 1700 Jahren]], about 1,700 years ago. A Roman decree from the year 321 mentions Jews in Cologne. In 2021, Germany marked the anniversary.

{Q66}

> SCENE (question): Question card #66.

[[Welche Städte haben die größten jüdischen Gemeinden in Deutschland?]] Which cities have the largest Jewish communities? [[Berlin und München]].

The trap is [[Worms und Speyer]]: famous in Jewish history, home to some of the oldest communities in Europe, but small today.

{Q184}

> SCENE (question): Question card #184.

[[Auf welcher rechtlichen Grundlage wurde der Staat Israel gegründet?]] On what legal basis was the state of Israel founded?

[[eine Resolution der Vereinten Nationen]], a United Nations resolution: the partition plan of November 1947. Israel declared independence in May 1948.

## What to remember for the exam

> SCENE (list): Recap board. Three cards appear one by one.
> 16 · Republik · Bund | → the name says it all @ One.
> Legislative, Exekutive, Judikative | → the press is not one of them @ Two.
> Bund, Länder, Gemeinden | → Ordnungsamt at the bottom @ Three.

[warm] Three things to take away. One: the name. A federal republic of sixteen states.

Two: three powers, legislative, executive, judicial. The press watches them; it isn't one of them.

Three: three levels, federation, states, municipalities. Your town hall is where the [[Ordnungsamt]] lives.

And the words to recognise on the day: [[Bundesrepublik]]. [[Bundesland]]. [[Gewaltenteilung]], the separation of powers. [[Bundespräsident]]. [[Bundeskanzler]]. [[Adler]]. [[Gemeinde]]. [[Widerspruch]].
