## Cook's cabbage

> SCENE (photo): The ship.
> cook-hook | Cook ranked something else first | color

[curious] Captain Cook reported three years round the world without one death from scurvy. Sauerkraut gets the credit. Cook ranked something else first.

> SCENE (term): Scurvy.
> Scurvy | a disease from too little vitamin C | Vitamin C is found in fresh fruit and vegetables (NHS)

[calm] Scurvy is a disease from too little vitamin C, the stuff in fresh fruit and vegetables. On long voyages, with no fresh food, sailors simply fell ill.

> SCENE (photo): The barrel.
> kraut-barrel | Sauerkraut: salted cabbage, soured on purpose | color

[warmly] Sauerkraut is cabbage, salted and left in a barrel. Bacteria eat its sugar and make acid. Sour on purpose, and it keeps for months.

> SCENE (bars): Vitamin C.
> Vitamin C in 100 g | US food database (USDA FoodData Central), milligrams
> Raw cabbage | 36.6 @ thirty-six point six
> Canned sauerkraut | 14.7 | red @ fourteen point seven

[curious] Here's the twist. Sauerkraut has less vitamin C than the raw cabbage it came from. The US food database lists thirty-six point six for raw cabbage, fourteen point seven for canned sauerkraut.

[curious] So did it really save the crew?

## The rye loaf

> SCENE (photo): The rye loaf.
> rye-loaf | German rye bread needs sour | color

[curious] Now the same sour trick, in a kitchen near you. German rye bread cannot be baked without it. Why not?

> SCENE (term): Sourdough.
> Sourdough | flour and water, soured by bacteria and lifted by yeast | Kept alive by feeding it flour and water (Spektrum)

[calm] First, what is sourdough? Flour and water, left alone. Bacteria make acid, yeast makes bubbles. Keep feeding it, and it never dies.

> SCENE (photo): Wheat dough.
> wheat-dough | Wheat has gluten | color

[calm] Wheat dough has gluten, a stretchy protein net that traps the bubbles. Rye flour has almost none of that.

> SCENE (bars): Rye's substitute.
> Slimy fibres (pentosans) in flour, % | Ernährungs-Umschau 9/2010
> Rye flour | 5 @ slimy, water-hungry fibres
> Wheat flour (about half) | 2.5 @ twice as much as wheat

[calm] Rye has a substitute: slimy, water-hungry fibres, about twice as much as wheat. But acid changes how they swell, and only then can they hold water and gas.

> SCENE (photo): Enzymes.
> flour-dough | Enzymes chop the starch | color

[curious] Second problem: rye flour is full of enzymes that chop starch apart. Starch is the dough's skeleton. Without acid, they eat it, and the loaf collapses.

> SCENE (receipt): The encyclopedia.
> bg | bg-library
> Lexikon der Ernährung, Spektrum | Sauerteig
> Roggenmehl wird =="erst durch Säuerung"== des Teiges backfähig | rye flour only becomes bakeable once the dough is soured @ only becomes bakeable

[calm] A German nutrition encyclopedia says it flatly: rye flour only becomes bakeable once the dough is soured.

> SCENE (term): Sauer.
> Sauer | from a root meaning sour, salty, even wet | Kraut: the dictionary says its origin is unclear (DWDS)

[curious] Word lab. Sauer comes from a very old root that meant sour, salty, even wet. And Kraut, the DWDS dictionary says, has an unclear origin. Even the cabbage's name is a mystery.

> SCENE (photo): Wholegrain rye.
> rye-slices | Calcium and iron, freed | color

[warmly] One more thing. In wholegrain rye, the sour dough also frees calcium and iron that the grain keeps locked away.
