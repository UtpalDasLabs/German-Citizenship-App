## The rule

> SCENE (photo): The stall.
> glu-market-hook | The cup has a legal definition | color

[curious] The cup of Glühwein in your hand has a legal definition. And the law bans one ingredient.

> SCENE (term): Mulled wine.
> Glühwein = mulled wine | wine heated with spices, usually sweetened | The English name for the same drink

[calm] Glühwein is what English speakers call mulled wine: wine heated with spices, and usually sweetened.

> SCENE (receipt): The rulebook.
> bg | bg-typewriter
> Regulation (EU) 251/2014 | On aromatised wine products
> Glühwein has ==an entry of its own== | in Annex II @ an entry of its own

[calm] The rulebook is a European regulation from 2014, about aromatised wine products. Glühwein has an entry of its own.

> SCENE (photo): The pot.
> glu-pot-stir | Rules one and two | color

[calm] Rule one: only red or white wine. Rule two: mainly cinnamon and cloves.

> SCENE (bignums): Rule three.
> The legal minimum
> 7 % | alcohol, at least @ seven percent

[calm] Rule three: at least seven percent alcohol.

> SCENE (stamp): No water.
> No water | red

[amused] Rule four: no water.

> SCENE (photo): The top-up.
> topping-up | A pot topped up with water | color

[warmly] A pot topped up with water is, by this definition, no longer Glühwein.

> SCENE (receipt): White Glühwein.
> bg | bg-typewriter2
> Regulation (EU) 251/2014, Annex II | White version
> The name must say it, for example =="weiß"== | a word that shows white wine was used @ weiß

[calm] White Glühwein is allowed too. But the name has to say so, with a word like weiß.

> SCENE (term): Word lab.
> Glühwein | glühen + Wein | to glow + wine (DWDS)

[curious] Word lab. The dictionary DWDS splits it into glühen, to glow, and Wein. Glowing wine.

## The surprises

> SCENE (photo): The duck.
> glu-duck | Kalte Ente: cold duck | color

[amused] The same annex has a stranger entry. Kalte Ente. Cold duck.

> SCENE (receipt): Kalte Ente.
> bg | bg-archive
> Regulation (EU) 251/2014, Annex II | Kalte Ente
> Wine plus at least ==a quarter sparkling wine== | share of the finished drink @ quarter sparkling
> Natural ==lemon== or its extracts | @ lemon
> At least ==7 % alcohol== | @ seven percent

[calm] Wine mixed with at least a quarter sparkling wine, natural lemon, and at least seven percent alcohol.

> SCENE (stamp): Cold duck is law.
> Cold duck is law | red

[amused] A cold duck. In European law.

> SCENE (photo): May.
> woodland | Maiwein: woodruff must dominate | color

[warmly] May has its own entries. Maiwein must taste mainly of woodruff, a small woodland herb.

> SCENE (term): Same recipe, other names.
> Three more names | Viiniglögi · Vinglögg · Karštas vynas | The same Glühwein recipe, listed as separate entries in Annex II

[curious] And the Glühwein recipe appears a second time under three other names: Viiniglögi, Vinglögg and Karštas vynas.

> SCENE (receipt): The everyday version.
> bg | bg-paper
> DWDS, Glühwein | The dictionary's description
> ==Sweetened== wine, cinnamon, cloves | usually @ sweetened
> Drunk at ==Advent== Christmas markets | @ Advent

[calm] The dictionary describes the everyday version: sweetened wine, cinnamon, cloves, drunk at Advent Christmas markets.

## From medicine to market

> SCENE (line): The market drink.
> How did hot spiced wine become the market drink?

[curious] So how did a hot spiced wine become the Christmas market drink?

> SCENE (photo): Old.
> glu-history | Spiced wine is old | color

[calm] It's old. A popular history site says the Romans spiced wine with honey, and medieval nobles drank spiced wine as medicine.

> SCENE (receipt): The recipe.
> bg | bg-news
> wissen.de, popular history | A recipe like today's
> A ==Saxon nobleman== wrote one down in ==1843== | cinnamon, ginger, anise, nutmeg, cardamom, saffron @ eighteen forty-three

[calm] A recipe like today's is first written down in Germany in eighteen forty-three, says the same site, by a Saxon nobleman.

> SCENE (bignums): The bottle.
> Ready-made Glühwein (wissen.de)
> 1956 | first bottled, Augsburg @ nineteen fifty-six
> early 1960s | wine law changed, sugar allowed

[calm] In nineteen fifty-six a wine merchant in Augsburg sold the first ready-made Glühwein in bottles. He was fined, because sugaring wine was illegal.

> SCENE (term): Watch next.
> Watch next: the Advent video | Hidden Deutschland: the oldest Glühwein cup, about 1420 | We could not find a museum record for it

[warmly] One news article even names a silver cup from around fourteen twenty as the oldest Glühwein cup. We couldn't find a museum record. Our Advent video tells that story, so watch it there.

> SCENE (receipt): The season.
> bg | bg-library
> Delivery service Flink, orders before 1 December 2024 | Glühwein
> Orders ==rose from November== | @ November
> ==Peak: 14 December== | @ fourteenth of December
> ==Sharp fall after 24 December== | @ Christmas Eve

[curious] And the season? One delivery service tracked its orders in 2024. Glühwein climbed from November, peaked on the fourteenth of December, and fell sharply after Christmas Eve.

> SCENE (photo): After Christmas.
> glu-after | The markets close | color

[calm] The shelves follow. From the twenty-seventh of December, that company marked left-over Christmas products down by up to thirty percent.

> SCENE (bignums): The markets matter.
> Fruit Glühwein sales (VdFw)
> 9.4 M | million litres a year before the pandemic @ nine point four
> 6.8 M | million litres in 2020, markets cancelled @ six point eight

[curious] And the markets matter. In 2020, with the markets cancelled, fruit Glühwein sales fell from about nine point four million litres to six point eight.

> SCENE (term): Official drink.
> 29 % | pick Glühwein as the official Christmas drink | YouGov, 1,000 Germans, autumn 2025; hot chocolate 15 %

[calm] Today, twenty-nine percent of Germans asked pick Glühwein as the official Christmas drink. Hot chocolate gets fifteen.

[calm] No rule we found ends it. The markets and the calendar do.
