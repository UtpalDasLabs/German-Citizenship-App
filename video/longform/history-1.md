## Cold open

> SCENE (fact): A date, alone on a dark screen.
> 30 JANUARY 1933 | Berlin | Adolf Hitler is appointed Reichskanzler, head of the government.

[dramatic] On the thirtieth of January 1933, Adolf Hitler became head of the German government. Within months, Germany's first democracy was gone. Within twelve years, millions of people had been murdered, and Europe was in ruins.

[dramatic] This part of the exam asks about those twelve years, and about how Germany remembers them today. Nineteen questions. Some are just dates. Some are the reason modern Germany is built the way it is.

> SCENE (title): Title card.
> GESCHICHTE I

## How a democracy ended

{Q156}

> SCENE (question): Question card #156. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] The first date to know. [[In welchem Jahr wurde Hitler Reichskanzler?]] In which year did Hitler become chancellor?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[1933]]. He was appointed by the president, Paul von Hindenburg, in January. He didn't seize power in a coup. He was handed it.

{Q152}

> SCENE (question): Question card #152. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Wann waren die Nationalsozialisten mit Adolf Hitler in Deutschland an der Macht?]] When were the National Socialists in power?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[1933 bis 1945]]. Twelve years, from Hitler's appointment to the end of the war.

{Q155}

> SCENE (question): Question card #155. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] The same question comes back with different options. [[Wann waren die Nationalsozialisten in Deutschland an der Macht?]]

[countdown] [dramatic] [[1933 bis 1945]], again. Whatever the other dates are, that's the pair to look for.

{Q157}

> SCENE (question): Question card #157. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Die Nationalsozialisten mit Adolf Hitler errichteten 1933 in Deutschland …]] In 1933, the National Socialists set up…

[countdown] [dramatic] [[eine Diktatur.]] A dictatorship. And they did it fast. In March 1933, the parliament passed a law that let the government make laws without it. By the summer, every other party was banned or had dissolved itself.

> SCENE (list): What disappeared in 1933.
> Parteien | other parties: banned @ banned
> Presse | newspapers: censored @ censored
> Wahlen | elections: no longer free @ free elections

[dramatic] Other parties were banned. Newspapers were censored. Opponents were arrested without trial. And there were no free elections any more.

{Q170}

> SCENE (question): Question card #170. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] So the exam asks: [[Was gab es während der Zeit des Nationalsozialismus in Deutschland?]] What existed during the Nazi period?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[das Verbot von Parteien]], the banning of parties. The other options are all things the Nazis destroyed: freedom of the press, the free development of your personality, the protection of human dignity. Today, all three are basic rights.

{Q159}

> SCENE (question): Question card #159. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] And the reverse, with a [[nicht]]. [[Was gab es in Deutschland nicht während der Zeit des Nationalsozialismus?]] What did not exist during the Nazi period?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[freie Wahlen]], free elections. Censorship, arbitrary arrests and the persecution of Jews all did exist. The question tests whether you can tell a dictatorship from a democracy.

## The night the synagogues burned

> SCENE (photo): A Jewish-owned shop in Magdeburg, its windows smashed, the morning after the pogrom.
> pogrom-magdeburg | Magdeburg, 10 November 1938: a destroyed Jewish shop | paper

[dramatic] From 1933, Jews in Germany were pushed out of public life step by step: out of jobs, out of schools, and in 1935, by law, out of their rights as citizens. Then, in November 1938, came open violence.

{Q164}

> SCENE (question): Question card #164. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Was passierte am 9. November 1938 in Deutschland?]] What happened on the ninth of November 1938?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[Jüdische Geschäfte und Synagogen werden durch Nationalsozialisten und ihre Anhänger zerstört.]] Jewish shops and synagogues were destroyed by the Nazis and their supporters.

[dramatic] Across the country, synagogues were set on fire, and thousands of shops and homes were wrecked. Hundreds of people died, and around thirty thousand Jewish men were taken to concentration camps. In Germany it's remembered as the [[Pogromnacht]], the night of the pogrom.

{Q163}

> SCENE (question): Question card #163. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] The exam also asks for the year on its own. [[In welchem Jahr zerstörten die Nationalsozialisten Synagogen und jüdische Geschäfte in Deutschland?]]

[countdown] [dramatic] [[1938]]. One year before the war began.

## The war

> SCENE (stat): Six years of war.
> 1939 | Germany attacks Poland @ 1939
> 1945 | Germany surrenders @ 1945

[dramatic] On the first of September 1939, Germany attacked Poland. Britain and France declared war two days later. It became the deadliest war in history.

{Q160}

> SCENE (question): Question card #160. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Welcher Krieg dauerte von 1939 bis 1945?]] Which war lasted from 1939 to 1945?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[der Zweite Weltkrieg]], the Second World War. Behind the front lines, the Nazi state murdered six million Jews in what is now called the Holocaust, along with Sinti and Roma, people with disabilities, and many others.

{Q162}

> SCENE (question): Question card #162. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] Not everyone went along with it. [[Claus Schenk Graf von Stauffenberg wurde bekannt durch …]] Claus von Stauffenberg became known for…

[countdown] [dramatic] [[das Attentat auf Hitler am 20. Juli 1944.]] The attempt on Hitler's life on the twentieth of July 1944. Stauffenberg, an army officer, placed a bomb in Hitler's headquarters. Hitler survived. Stauffenberg was shot the same night in Berlin. Today, the twentieth of July is a day of remembrance for the German resistance.

{Q153}

> SCENE (question): Question card #153. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Was war am 8. Mai 1945?]] What happened on the eighth of May 1945?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[Ende des Zweiten Weltkriegs in Europa]], the end of the Second World War in Europe.

[dramatic] Watch out for the trap here: Hitler's death. He killed himself on the thirtieth of April, a week earlier. The war didn't end with him.

{Q179}

> SCENE (question): Question card #179. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Wie endete der Zweite Weltkrieg in Europa offiziell?]] How did the war in Europe officially end?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[durch die bedingungslose Kapitulation Deutschlands]], through Germany's unconditional surrender. Not a revolution, not a retreat. The German army surrendered, without conditions.

{Q154}

> SCENE (question): Question card #154. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] And the simplest version: [[Wann war der Zweite Weltkrieg zu Ende?]]

[countdown] [dramatic] [[1945]]. In Europe in May; in Asia, with Japan's surrender, in September.

## How Germany remembers

> SCENE (term): Remembrance, as a word and a duty.
> Erinnerungskultur | the culture of remembrance | Gedenken

[dramatic] After 1945, Germany had to decide how to live with what it had done. The answer it chose was to remember, in public, every year. The Germans even have a word for it: [[Erinnerungskultur]], the culture of remembrance.

{Q220}

> SCENE (question): Question card #220. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Der 27. Januar ist in Deutschland ein offizieller Gedenktag. Woran erinnert dieser Tag?]] The twenty-seventh of January is an official day of remembrance. What does it remember?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[an die Opfer des Nationalsozialismus, Tag der Befreiung des Vernichtungslagers Auschwitz]]. The victims of National Socialism. On the twenty-seventh of January 1945, the Soviet army liberated Auschwitz, the largest of the death camps.

> SCENE (photo): Brass plates set into a pavement in front of a house.
> stolpersteine | Stolpersteine in Berlin: "Hier wohnte …", here lived… | color

[dramatic] And there's a way of remembering you'll see on ordinary streets. Small brass plates, set into the pavement in front of houses. Each one starts with the words [[Hier wohnte]], here lived, then a name, and what happened to that person.

{Q206}

> SCENE (question): Question card #206. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Woran erinnern die sogenannten „Stolpersteine" in Deutschland?]] What do the so-called Stolpersteine remember?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[an die Opfer des Nationalsozialismus]], the victims of National Socialism. The artist Gunter Demnig began laying them in the early nineteen-nineties. There are now more than a hundred thousand, across Europe. The name means "stumbling stones": you come across them in the middle of everyday life.

{Q96}

> SCENE (question): Question card #96. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] Remembering also means protecting the facts. [[Wie kann jemand, der den Holocaust leugnet, bestraft werden?]] How can someone who denies the Holocaust be punished?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[mit Freiheitsstrafe bis zu fünf Jahren oder mit Geldstrafe]], with up to five years in prison, or a fine. In Germany, denying the Holocaust is a crime.

{Q149}

> SCENE (question): Question card #149. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Was ist ein Beispiel für antisemitisches Verhalten?]] What is an example of antisemitic behaviour?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[den Holocaust leugnen]], denying the Holocaust. The other options aren't antisemitic: visiting a Jewish festival, playing football against a Jewish team, or criticising the Israeli government, like you can criticise any government.

{Q14}

> SCENE (question): Question card #14. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] The same idea shapes how far free speech goes. [[Meinungsfreiheit in Deutschland heißt, dass ich …]] Freedom of expression in Germany means that I…

[countdown] [dramatic] [[meine Meinung im Internet äußern kann.]] Can say what I think online. Insulting people in the street isn't covered. And showing Nazi symbols in public is a crime, and so are the symbols of Hamas and the so-called Islamic State.

{Q288}

> SCENE (question): Question card #288. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] One last question. [[Woraus begründet sich Deutschlands besondere Verantwortung für Israel?]] What is Germany's special responsibility for Israel based on?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[aus den nationalsozialistischen Verbrechen gegen Juden]], the Nazi crimes against Jews. Not EU membership, not the Basic Law's text, not Christian tradition. It comes from history.
