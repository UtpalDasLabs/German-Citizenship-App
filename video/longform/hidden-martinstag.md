## The soldier and the beggar

> SCENE (photo): El Greco's painting, then the soldier at the gate.
> elgreco | El Greco, Saint Martin and the Beggar, 1597–1599 | color from 0.5 0.35 1.0 to 0.5 0.4 1.12

[warmly] Martin was a Roman soldier, in the fourth century. The story goes that on a freezing night, he rode past a beggar at the gate of a town. He had nothing to give, except his cloak.

> SCENE (photo): The cloak cut in two.
> cloak-b | He gave the beggar half. | color key

[warmly] So he took his sword, cut the cloak in two, and gave the beggar half.

> SCENE (photo): Candles; Martin's tomb in Tours.
> candles |  | color
> tours | Martin's tomb, Tours, France | color @ bishop of Tours

[warmly] That night, the legend says, Martin dreamed of Jesus, wearing the half cloak. He left the army, became a monk, and later the bishop of Tours, in France. He was buried on the eleventh of November, in the year 397. That's why his day is the eleventh.

## The lanterns

> SCENE (photo): A glowing lantern; the procession; the rider.
> lantern |  | color
> lantern-walk |  | color @ make their own lanterns
> lantern-walk-b |  | color @ rides ahead

[calm] Today, the [[Laternenumzug]], the lantern procession, is the part most people see. Children make their own lanterns, often at kindergarten or school, and walk together after dark, singing songs like [[Laterne, Laterne]]. In many towns, someone dressed as Martin rides ahead on a horse.

> SCENE (photo): A bonfire; rain on a window at night.
> bonfire | Martin's kindness brought light into the dark. | color key
> rain-night |  | color @ dark by five

[calm] At the end, there's often a [[Martinsfeuer]], a big fire, and the cloak scene is acted out. The light is the whole point. Martin's kindness, the idea goes, brought light into the dark. And in November in Germany, it's dark by five.

## The goose

> SCENE (photo): Martin hiding among the geese.
> geese-barn |  | color

[calm] Why goose? There's a legend for that too. When the people of Tours wanted Martin as their bishop, he didn't want the job. So he hid in a barn full of geese. The geese honked so loudly that he was found, and made bishop anyway. The goose on your plate, the story says, is their punishment.

> SCENE (photo): Geese in a field, then a goose dinner.
> geese-field |  | color
> goose | Martini: the old day for paying rents and taxes. | color key @ Many people paid

The real reason is more practical. [[Martini]], the eleventh of November, was an old date for paying rents, taxes and wages, after the harvest. Many people paid in kind. And a goose, fattened over the summer, was a common way to pay.

## Why Karneval starts on 11.11

> SCENE (list): From 11 November to Christmas: a time of fasting.
> bg | candles
> 11 November | Saint Martin's Day
> Until Christmas | weeks of fasting @ time of fasting
> The day itself | one last feast @ Saint Martin's Lent

[warmly] Here's the part almost nobody knows. In the Middle Ages, the weeks from Saint Martin's Day to Christmas were a time of fasting, a bit like Lent before Easter. It was even called Saint Martin's Lent.

> SCENE (photo): A fire, a goose dinner, confetti.
> fire2 |  | color
> goose |  | color @ Eat the goose
> confetti |  | color @ Celebrate

[calm] So the eleventh of November was the last big feast before weeks of going without. Eat the goose. Finish the meat. Celebrate.

> SCENE (place): Düsseldorf on 11 November.
> carnival | COLOGNE · DÜSSELDORF · MAINZ | 11 November, 11:11

[calm] A last party before the fast. That's exactly what Karneval is. So when Cologne, Düsseldorf and Mainz open the carnival season on the eleventh of the eleventh, at eleven minutes past eleven, they're building on that old feast day. And the eleven? It's the fool's number. One more than the Ten Commandments, one less than the twelve apostles.

## Sweet bread men

> SCENE (photo): Dough being shaped; a Weckmann.
> dough |  | color
> weckmann | Weckmann · Stutenkerl | color key @ In the Rhineland

Around the same time, bakeries sell little men made of sweet bread. In the Rhineland, they're called a [[Weckmann]]. In Westphalia, a [[Stutenkerl]]. Same little man, a different name almost everywhere you go.

## How to join

> SCENE (photo): A lantern; the procession.
> lantern | Search your town for "Martinsumzug" or "Laternenumzug". | color key
> lantern-walk-b |  | color @ Bring a lantern

You don't need to be religious, German, or a child to take part. Kindergartens, schools and churches organise most processions, and many are open to everyone. Look on your town's website for [[Martinsumzug]] or [[Laternenumzug]]. Bring a lantern. Learn the first line of [[Laterne, Laterne]]. That's all.
