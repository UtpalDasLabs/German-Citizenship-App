## The evidence

> The Garden of Delights, made for the nuns of Hohenburg, Alsace @ painting from Alsace

[calm] The oldest evidence we found is a twelfth-century painting from Alsace: a pretzel-shaped bread at a banquet. [warmly] But the original burned in 1870, we only have copies, and a picture shows a shape. It can't show lye.

## The tray

> SCENE (photo): Lye and the tray.
> tray-lye |  | color

[calm] So we can't prove who. But we can prove this: lye eats aluminium, and an aluminium tray gives metal to the pretzel.

> SCENE (alubars): The bars.

[calm] Inspectors in North Rhine-Westphalia measured aluminium in lye pastry. On average, five to nine milligrams per kilo. The worst sample? Eighty-three. Is that a problem?

> SCENE (bignums): One pretzel.
> One 40 g pretzel (the bakers' association's average weight)
> <0.5 mg | at the average @ At the average
> 3.3 mg | at the worst @ At the worst

[calm] Put that in one pretzel. At the average, under half a milligram. At the worst, three point three.

> SCENE (receipt): Then and now.
> BfR, 2002, and the GDCh working paper, 2015 | Then and now
> 2002: not a concern, against a ==1989 limit== | 7 mg per kg of body weight per week @ no concern
> Today: EFSA limit ==1 mg== per kg per week | 2015 paper: a worst-case pretzel can reach or slightly pass a 20 kg child's day-share @ the limit came down
> Still judged harmless by current knowledge. Extra intake: unwanted. @ harmless by current knowledge

[calm] In 2002 Germany's risk institute, the BfR, called this no concern, by the limit of the day. Then the limit came down. By 2015, chemists calculated that one worst-case pretzel could reach a small child's daily share. [warmly] Still harmless by current knowledge, they said, but extra intake is unwanted.

## On your plate

> SCENE (saltgauge): The salt gauge.

[amused] One more number. A frozen pretzel already holds a quarter of the German Nutrition Society's daily salt maximum, before the coarse salt on top. With it, more than half.

[curious] And why does it smell like that? Food chemists in Munich tested 28 smell compounds in the crust. Two stood out: one like caramel, one like popcorn.
