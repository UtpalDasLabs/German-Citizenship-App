## Why it works

> SCENE (photo): The pretzel.
> pretzel-hook | Also in drain cleaner | color

[curious] A classic German pretzel is dipped in a chemical that's also in drain cleaner. On purpose.

> SCENE (term): Lauge.
> Lauge | lye: a strong alkali (the opposite of an acid) in water | Old High German louga, related to Latin lavare, "to wash"

[calm] That chemical is lye. In German, Lauge: a strong alkali, the opposite of an acid, in water. The word is related to Latin lavare, to wash. So technically, your pretzel gets a wash.

> SCENE (photo): The dip.
> lye-dip |  | color

[warmly] Don't panic. It's the food-grade kind, a few percent strong, one quick dip. But the real surprise was hiding somewhere else.

> SCENE (bignums): The smell.
> bg | lab-molecules
> The smell of a pretzel lives in the crust
> 28 | smell compounds @ 28 smell compounds
> 2 | caramel and popcorn @ Two stood out

[curious] Why dip at all? The smell lives in the crust. Munich food chemists found 28 smell compounds there. Two stood out: caramel and popcorn. Lye is part of the reason.

## The evidence

> SCENE (figure): The Hortus Deliciarum, the banquet of Esther.
> hortus | Hortus Deliciarum, late 12th century | 0.60 0.545 @ pretzel-shaped bread
> The Garden of Delights, made for the nuns of Hohenburg, Alsace @ book from Alsace
> The original burned in 1870. Only copies survive. @ burned in 1870

[calm] The oldest evidence we found is a twelfth-century book from Alsace, on the French border. In a banquet scene, a pretzel-shaped bread. [warmly] But the original burned in 1870, we only have copies, and a picture shows a shape. It can't show lye.

> SCENE (term): Brezel.
> Brezel | from Latin bracchium, "arm" | Oldest form brezzita, 9th century: a sweet, twisted pastry (DWDS)

[curious] And the oldest word? Brezel comes from Latin bracchium: arm. Think folded arms. Its oldest known form, from the ninth century, meant a sweet, twisted pastry. Nothing about lye.

## The tray

> SCENE (photo): Lye and the tray.
> tray-lye |  | color

[calm] So both say nothing about lye. But we can prove this: lye eats aluminium, and an aluminium tray gives metal to the pretzel.

> SCENE (alubars): The bars.

[calm] Inspectors in North Rhine-Westphalia measured aluminium in lye pastry. On average, five to nine milligrams per kilo of pastry. A milligram is a thousandth of a gram. The worst sample? Eighty-three. Is that a problem?

> SCENE (bignums): One pretzel.
> One 40 g pretzel (the bakers' association's average weight)
> <0.5 mg | at the average @ At the average
> 3.3 mg | at the worst @ At the worst

[calm] Put that in one pretzel. At the average, under half a milligram. At the worst, three point three.

> SCENE (receipt): The BfR statement.
> bg | bg-paper
> BfR, German Federal Institute for Risk Assessment | Statement of 25 November 2002
> 2002: not a concern, against a ==1989 limit== | 7 mg per kg of body weight per week @ no concern
> Then the limit came down: EFSA, today ==1 mg== per kg per week | 2015 paper: a worst-case pretzel can reach or slightly pass a 20 kg child's day-share @ limit came down

[calm] In 2002 Germany's food-safety institute, the BfR, called this no concern, by the limit of the day. Then the limit came down.

> SCENE (childgauge): The child's share.

[calm] By 2015, German food chemists calculated that one worst-case pretzel could reach a small child's daily share. They still call it harmless by current knowledge, but say extra intake is unwanted.

> SCENE (photo): Steel trays or baking paper.
> tray-swap |  | color

[warmly] And the fix is almost boring. Stainless steel trays, or baking paper.

> SCENE (receipt): Is it fixed?
> bg | bg-dough
> Stiftung Warentest, 22 February 2017 | 17 pretzels tested
> ...Inzwischen ==nutzen viele Hersteller Edelstahlbleche== | many manufacturers now use stainless steel @ stainless steel
> The tested pretzels: ==low aluminium== | we have no recent numbers of our own @ came out low

[calm] Is it fixed? In 2017, the consumer-testing foundation Stiftung Warentest said many bakers now use stainless steel, and the pretzels it tested came out low. We have no recent numbers ourselves.
