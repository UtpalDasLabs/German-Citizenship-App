## Cold open

> SCENE (quote): An East German official at a press conference, reading from a note.
> Das tritt nach meiner Kenntnis … ist das sofort, unverzüglich. | As far as I know, that takes effect… immediately, without delay. | Günter Schabowski, 9 November 1989

[dramatic] On the evening of the ninth of November 1989, a government spokesman in East Berlin read out new travel rules at a press conference. A journalist asked when they would start. He looked at his notes and said: [[sofort, unverzüglich]]. Immediately.

[dramatic] He wasn't supposed to say that. But it was on television, and within hours, thousands of people were at the border crossings in Berlin. Shortly before midnight, the guards opened the gates. This lesson is how that night came about, and what happened next. Twenty-five exam questions.

> SCENE (title): Title card.
> GESCHICHTE III

## Life in the DDR

{Q187}

> SCENE (question): Question card #187: a black, red and gold flag with an emblem in the middle. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] First, recognise the state. The exam shows a flag: black, red and gold, with a hammer, a compass and a ring of wheat in the middle. [[Welcher deutsche Staat hatte eine schwarz-rot-goldene Flagge mit Hammer, Zirkel und Ährenkranz?]]

[countdown] [dramatic] [[DDR]]. The hammer stood for the workers, the compass for the engineers and scientists, the wheat for the farmers. The Federal Republic used the same colours, without the emblem.

{Q209}

> SCENE (question): Question card #209: four coats of arms. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] The emblem on its own comes up too. [[Welches war das Wappen der Deutschen Demokratischen Republik?]] Which was the coat of arms of the DDR? Look for the hammer and compass.

[countdown] [dramatic] [[Bild 4]], picture four. Picture one, the eagle, is the Federal Republic. Two is an old Christian symbol, and three is the cross of the Bundeswehr, the German army. Hammer and compass mean the DDR.

> SCENE (term): The ministry everyone feared.
> Stasi | Ministerium für Staatssicherheit | DDR, 1950 – 1990

[dramatic] The DDR held on to power with the help of a secret police that watched its own people. In 1989 it had more than ninety thousand full-time staff, and close to two hundred thousand unofficial informers: neighbours, colleagues, sometimes family.

{Q199}

> SCENE (question): Question card #199. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Mit der Abkürzung "Stasi" meinte man in der DDR …]] In the DDR, "Stasi" meant…

[countdown] [dramatic] [[das Ministerium für Staatssicherheit.]] The Ministry for State Security. Stasi is short for Staatssicherheit.

{Q208}

> SCENE (question): Question card #208. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Was war die "Stasi"?]] What was the Stasi?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[der Geheimdienst der DDR]], the DDR's secret service. The trap is the secret police of the Third Reich. That was the Gestapo, a different time and a different state.

{Q298}

> SCENE (question): Question card #298. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] The DDR also brought in workers from abroad. [[In der DDR lebten vor allem Migrantinnen und Migranten aus …]] Migrants in the DDR came mainly from…

[countdown] [dramatic] [[Vietnam, Polen, Mosambik.]] Vietnam, Poland and Mozambique: friendly socialist states that sent contract workers. The largest group, by far, came from Vietnam.

## Reaching out to the East

> SCENE (fact): A chancellor kneels.
> 7 DECEMBER 1970 | Warschau | Willy Brandt kneels at the memorial to the Warsaw Ghetto Uprising.

[dramatic] In the West, a new chancellor, Willy Brandt, tried something different: talking to the East instead of ignoring it. And in December 1970, on a visit to Warsaw, he did something nobody expected.

{Q181}

> SCENE (question): Question card #181: the photo of Brandt kneeling in Warsaw, described. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] The exam shows the photo. [[Was wollte Willy Brandt mit seinem Kniefall 1970 im ehemaligen jüdischen Ghetto in Warschau ausdrücken?]] What did Brandt want to express by kneeling?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[Er bat Polen und die polnischen Juden um Vergebung.]] He was asking Poland and the Polish Jews for forgiveness. He knelt at the memorial to the Jews who rose up against the Nazis in the Warsaw Ghetto in 1943. He hadn't planned it, and he said nothing.

{Q211}

> SCENE (question): Question card #211. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Welcher Politiker steht für die "Ostverträge"?]] Which politician stands for the "Eastern treaties"?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[Willy Brandt]]. Treaties with the Soviet Union, Poland, the DDR and Czechoslovakia, signed between 1970 and 1973. He won the Nobel Peace Prize for that policy in 1971.

## Autumn 1989

> SCENE (photo): A crowd with banners fills a Leipzig street on a Monday evening.
> leipzig-1989 | Leipzig, October 1989: a Monday demonstration | paper

[dramatic] By 1989, change was sweeping Eastern Europe. In the DDR, people began meeting every Monday after prayers at a church in Leipzig, then walking through the city. On the ninth of October, seventy thousand of them marched, peacefully, past armed police. Nobody fired.

{Q166}

> SCENE (question): Question card #166. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] Their slogan is an exam question. [[Bei welchen Demonstrationen in Deutschland riefen die Menschen "Wir sind das Volk"?]] At which demonstrations did people shout "We are the people"?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[bei den Montagsdemonstrationen 1989 in der DDR]], the Monday demonstrations of 1989 in the DDR. Four words that told a one-party state who it actually answered to.

{Q196}

> SCENE (question): Question card #196. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Warum nennt man die Zeit im Herbst 1989 in der DDR "Die Wende"? In dieser Zeit veränderte sich die DDR politisch …]] Why is autumn 1989 called "the turn"? The DDR changed politically…

[countdown] [dramatic] [[von einer Diktatur zur Demokratie.]] From a dictatorship to a democracy. In March 1990, East Germans voted in the first free election in the DDR's history.

{Q191}

> SCENE (question): Question card #191. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Wann wurde die Mauer in Berlin für alle geöffnet?]] When was the Wall opened for everyone?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[1989]]. The ninth of November: the night from the start of this lesson.

## One country again

> SCENE (fact): The date Germany became one country again.
> 3 OCTOBER 1990 | Berlin | The DDR joins the Federal Republic. Germany is one country again.

[dramatic] Less than a year after the Wall opened, on the third of October 1990, the two German states became one.

{Q204}

> SCENE (question): Question card #204. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Wie wurden die Bundesrepublik Deutschland und die DDR zu einem Staat?]] How did the two become one state?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[Die heutigen fünf östlichen Bundesländer sind der Bundesrepublik Deutschland beigetreten.]] Today's five eastern states joined the Federal Republic. Nobody occupied anybody. The East joined the West, under the Basic Law, which stayed the constitution for everyone.

{Q228}

> SCENE (question): Question card #228. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Wie wird der Beitritt der DDR zur Bundesrepublik Deutschland im Jahr 1990 allgemein genannt?]] What is that accession usually called?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[Deutsche Wiedervereinigung]], German reunification. The other options are about NATO and the EU growing east, which happened later.

{Q218}

> SCENE (question): Question card #218. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Wie viele Bundesländer kamen bei der Wiedervereinigung 1990 zur Bundesrepublik Deutschland hinzu?]] How many states joined in 1990?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[5]]. Five new states. And East Berlin joined West Berlin, so Berlin became one city again.

{Q194}

> SCENE (question): Question card #194. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Am 3. Oktober feiert man in Deutschland den Tag der Deutschen …]] On the third of October, Germany celebrates the Day of German…

[countdown] [dramatic] [[Einheit.]] Unity. It's the national holiday, and it replaced the seventeenth of June from the last lesson.

{Q215}

> SCENE (question): Question card #215. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Wer wird als "Kanzler der Deutschen Einheit" bezeichnet?]] Who is called the "chancellor of German unity"?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[Helmut Kohl]]. He was chancellor from 1982 to 1998, and he negotiated reunification with the four former occupying powers.

{Q219}

> SCENE (question): Question card #219. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Die Bundesrepublik Deutschland hat die Grenzen von heute seit …]] Germany has had today's borders since…

[countdown] [dramatic] [[1990]]. In a treaty that year, the two German states and the four powers settled Germany's borders for good, including the border with Poland.

{Q205}

> SCENE (question): Question card #205. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Mit dem Beitritt der DDR zur Bundesrepublik Deutschland gehören die neuen Bundesländer nun auch …]] With reunification, the new states now also belong…

[countdown] [dramatic] [[zur Europäischen Union.]] To the European Union. Overnight, the former DDR left the Warsaw Pact's world and became part of the European Community, today's EU.

## The five new states

> SCENE (states): The five states that were once the DDR light up.
> Mecklenburg-Vorpommern, Brandenburg, Sachsen, Sachsen-Anhalt, Thüringen | Die fünf neuen Länder @ five

[dramatic] The exam asks five times which state used to be part of the DDR. So learn the five: [[Mecklenburg-Vorpommern]] in the north. [[Brandenburg]] around Berlin. [[Sachsen]] in the south-east. [[Sachsen-Anhalt]] in the middle. And [[Thüringen]] in the south.

## Germany today

{Q64}

> SCENE (question): Question card #64. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] Two questions about the country that came out of all this. [[Die Bundesrepublik Deutschland ist heute gegliedert in …]] Today, Germany is divided into…

[countdown] [dramatic] [[Bund, Länder und Kommunen.]] The federation, the states and the local authorities. No more zones, no more East and West, and no cantons: those are Swiss.

{Q102}

> SCENE (question): Question card #102. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Womit kann man in der Bundesrepublik Deutschland geehrt werden, wenn man auf politischem, wirtschaftlichem, kulturellem, geistigem oder sozialem Gebiet eine besondere Leistung erbracht hat? Mit dem …]] How can someone be honoured for outstanding service to the country?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[Bundesverdienstkreuz]], the Federal Cross of Merit. The federal president awards it. The trap options are honours of the DDR, a state that no longer exists.
