## Cold open

> SCENE (fact): A date, alone on a dark screen.
> 13 AUGUST 1961 | Berlin | Overnight, soldiers and workers close the border through the middle of the city.

[dramatic] On the morning of the thirteenth of August 1961, people in Berlin woke up to barbed wire across their streets. Families on one side, jobs on the other. Within days, the wire became a wall. It stood for twenty-eight years.

[dramatic] How did one city, and one country, end up cut in two? That's this lesson: from the end of the war to the Berlin Wall. Twenty-three exam questions along the way.

> SCENE (title): Title card.
> GESCHICHTE II

## Four zones

> SCENE (list): The four powers that occupied Germany.
> USA | the south @ USA
> Sowjetunion | the east @ Soviet Union
> Großbritannien | the north-west @ Britain
> Frankreich | the south-west @ France

[dramatic] In May 1945, Germany had no government of its own. The four Allied powers took over: the USA, the Soviet Union, Britain and France. They split the country into zones, one each.

[dramatic] [[Wie viele Besatzungszonen gab es in Deutschland nach dem Zweiten Weltkrieg?]] How many occupation zones were there?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[4]]. One for each of the four powers.

[countdown] [dramatic] [[USA, Sowjetunion, Großbritannien, Frankreich]]. Every wrong option slips in a country that wasn't one of them: Poland, Sweden, Italy, Japan, Spain, Portugal.

[countdown] [dramatic] [[Japan]]. Japan fought on Germany's side in the war. It never occupied anything in Germany.

{Q176}

> SCENE (question): Question card #176: the map with four numbered zones. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.
> @ One is Britain

[dramatic] The exam shows a map with four numbered zones. [[Wie waren die Besatzungszonen Deutschlands nach 1945 verteilt?]] How were the zones divided up?

[countdown] [dramatic] One is Britain, in the north-west. Two, the Soviet Union, in the east. Three, the USA, in the south. And four, France, in the south-west, on its own border. Remember it as a compass: Britain north, Soviets east, Americans south, French west.

{Q177}

> SCENE (question): Question card #177. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] The capital got the same treatment. [[Welche deutsche Stadt wurde nach dem Zweiten Weltkrieg in vier Sektoren aufgeteilt?]] Which city was divided into four sectors?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[Berlin]]. And here's the problem: Berlin lay deep inside the Soviet zone. The three western sectors were an island, more than a hundred and fifty kilometres from the nearest western zone.

## The airlift

> SCENE (photo): An American transport plane comes in low over Berlin rooftops to land at Tempelhof.
> airlift-tempelhof | Tempelhof airport, Berlin, 1948: a C-54 comes in to land | paper

[dramatic] In June 1948, the western zones brought in a new currency, the Deutsche Mark. The Soviet Union answered by cutting off West Berlin: no trains, no trucks, no barges. Two million people, and food for a few weeks.

{Q178}

> SCENE (question): Question card #178. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Vom Juni 1948 bis zum Mai 1949 wurden die Bürgerinnen und Bürger von West-Berlin durch eine Luftbrücke versorgt. Welcher Umstand war dafür verantwortlich?]] West Berlin was supplied by an airlift. Why?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[Die Sowjetunion unterbrach den gesamten Verkehr auf dem Landwege.]] The Soviet Union blocked all traffic by land.

[dramatic] So the western Allies flew everything in: food, coal, medicine. At the peak, a plane landed almost every minute, day and night. Berliners called them [[Rosinenbomber]], raisin bombers, because some pilots dropped sweets for the children. In May 1949, the Soviet Union gave up and lifted the blockade.

## Two states in one year

> SCENE (list): Two states in one year.
> Bundesrepublik | 23 May 1949, in the three western zones @ In May
> DDR | 7 October 1949, in the Soviet zone @ In October

[dramatic] By then, the West and the Soviet Union could no longer agree on anything. So in 1949, two German states were founded. In May, in the three western zones, the Federal Republic. In October, in the Soviet zone, the German Democratic Republic.

> SCENE (question): Question card #169. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Wann wurde die Bundesrepublik Deutschland gegründet?]] When was the Federal Republic founded?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[1949]]. On the twenty-third of May, the day the Basic Law came into force.

{Q165}

> SCENE (question): Question card #165. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Wie hieß der erste Bundeskanzler der Bundesrepublik Deutschland?]] Who was the first federal chancellor?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[Konrad Adenauer]]. He was already seventy-three when he took office, and he stayed for fourteen years. Willy Brandt, Helmut Schmidt and Kurt Georg Kiesinger all came later.

> SCENE (question): Question card #180. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[countdown] [dramatic] [[Konrad Adenauer.]] Again. Ludwig Erhard was second.

> SCENE (question): Question card #174. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] Now the other state. [[Wann wurde die DDR gegründet?]] When was the DDR founded?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[1949]], too. On the seventh of October. Two states, one year.

{Q172}

> SCENE (question): Question card #172. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[In welcher Besatzungszone wurde die DDR gegründet? In der …]] In which occupation zone was the DDR founded?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[sowjetischen Besatzungszone]], the Soviet zone. The Soviet Union shaped the new state on its own model.

{Q190}

> SCENE (question): Question card #190. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Was bedeutet die Abkürzung DDR?]] What does DDR stand for?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[Deutsche Demokratische Republik]], German Democratic Republic. Despite the name, it was not a democracy. One party, the [[SED]], held all the power.

{Q217}

> SCENE (question): Question card #217. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[In welchem Zeitraum gab es die Deutsche Demokratische Republik?]] When did the DDR exist?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[1949 bis 1990]]. Forty-one years, from its founding to German reunification.

## East and West

> SCENE (quote): Churchill's phrase.
> Ein eiserner Vorhang ist über den Kontinent niedergegangen. | An iron curtain has descended across the Continent. | Winston Churchill, 1946

[dramatic] In 1946, Britain's wartime leader Winston Churchill described the new Europe: an iron curtain had come down across the continent. On one side, the Soviet Union and the states it controlled. On the other, the West.

{Q185}

> SCENE (question): Question card #185. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Wofür stand der Ausdruck "Eiserner Vorhang"? Für die Abschottung …]] What did "Iron Curtain" stand for? The sealing off…

[countdown] [dramatic] [[des Warschauer Pakts gegen den Westen]], of the Warsaw Pact from the West.

{Q202}

> SCENE (question): Question card #202. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Zu wem gehörte die DDR im "Kalten Krieg"?]] Which side was the DDR on in the Cold War?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[zum Warschauer Pakt]], the Warsaw Pact: the Soviet-led alliance of Eastern Europe, founded in 1955. West Germany joined NATO that same year.

{Q207}

> SCENE (question): Question card #207. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[In welchem Militärbündnis war die DDR Mitglied?]] Which military alliance was the DDR a member of?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[im Warschauer Pakt]]. Same answer, asked as a military question.

{Q203}

> SCENE (question): Question card #203. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] The two states also ran their economies in opposite ways. [[Wie hieß das Wirtschaftssystem der DDR?]] What was the DDR's economic system called?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[Planwirtschaft]], a planned economy. The state decided what was made, how much, and at what price, in five-year plans. The West had the social market economy, from the Work lesson.

## The uprising of 1953

> SCENE (fact): Workers on the march in East Berlin.
> 17 JUNE 1953 | DDR | Strikes and protests spread across the country. Soviet tanks put them down.

[dramatic] In June 1953, construction workers in East Berlin put down their tools. The government had raised their work quotas without raising their pay. Within a day, the strike had spread across the country, and people were demanding free elections.

{Q210}

> SCENE (question): Question card #210. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Was ereignete sich am 17. Juni 1953 in der DDR?]] What happened on the seventeenth of June 1953 in the DDR?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[landesweite Streiks und ein Volksaufstand]], nationwide strikes and a popular uprising. Soviet tanks crushed it the same day. Dozens of people were killed.

{Q186}

> SCENE (question): Question card #186. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Im Jahr 1953 gab es in der DDR einen Aufstand, an den lange Zeit in der Bundesrepublik Deutschland ein Feiertag erinnerte. Wann war das?]] West Germany remembered that uprising with a public holiday. Which date?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[17. Juni]], the seventeenth of June. Until 1990 it was a holiday in West Germany. The trap options are other famous dates: the first of May, the twentieth of July, the ninth of November.

## The Wall

> SCENE (quote): Walter Ulbricht at a press conference, June 1961.
> Niemand hat die Absicht, eine Mauer zu errichten. | Nobody intends to build a wall. | Walter Ulbricht, 15 June 1961

[dramatic] Through the nineteen-fifties, people kept leaving the DDR for the West, millions of them, most through the open border in Berlin. In June 1961, the DDR's leader Walter Ulbricht told journalists: [[Niemand hat die Absicht, eine Mauer zu errichten.]] Nobody intends to build a wall. Two months later, they built it.

> SCENE (photo): Workers raise the wall at Friedrichstraße, watched by armed guards.
> mauerbau | Berlin, Friedrichstraße, 1961: the Wall goes up | paper

{Q151}

> SCENE (question): Question card #151. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Wer baute die Mauer in Berlin?]] Who built the Wall in Berlin?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[die DDR]]. The East German state built it to stop its own people from leaving. It called it a protective wall against the West. The guards were ordered to shoot anyone who tried to cross. At least a hundred and forty people died at the Berlin Wall.

{Q188}

> SCENE (question): Question card #188. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[In welchem Jahr wurde die Mauer in Berlin gebaut?]] In which year was the Wall built?

[countdown] [dramatic] [[1961]]. The thirteenth of August.

{Q189}

> SCENE (question): Question card #189. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] Asked again, with different options: [[Wann baute die DDR die Mauer in Berlin?]]

[countdown] [dramatic] [[1961]]. Nineteen-nineteen, thirty-three and ninety are all real turning points in German history, just not this one.

{Q193}

> SCENE (question): Question card #193. Three seconds to guess, then the answer highlights.

[dramatic] [[Von 1961 bis 1989 war Berlin …]] From 1961 to 1989, Berlin was…

[countdown] [dramatic] [[durch eine Mauer geteilt.]] Divided by a wall. It still had mayors, it wasn't its own country, and you could reach it by road and rail. But it was a city cut in two.
